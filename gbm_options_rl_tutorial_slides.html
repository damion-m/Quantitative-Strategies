PART 01

Setup, data and the GBM model

GBM says log returns are normal and independent, with constant drift μ and volatility σ. Pricing, forecasts and the edge all rest on this assumption.

Cells 1–5
Part 1 · the model

Prices follow drift plus a shock that scales with the price

\[ dS_t = \mu S_t\,dt + \sigma S_t\,dW_t \]

W is Brownian motion: increments over dt are normal with mean 0 and variance dt. Itô's lemma on log S gives a closed form:

\[ S_T = S_0 \exp\!\Big(\big(\mu - \tfrac12\sigma^2\big)T + \sigma\sqrt{T}\,Z\Big),\quad Z\sim N(0,1) \]

The log return is normal, so the price is lognormal: never negative, skewed right.

\[ \ln\tfrac{S_T}{S_0} \sim N\big((\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)T,\ \sigma^2T\big),\qquad \mathbb{E}[S_T]=S_0e^{\mu T} \]

Volatility drag: the median grows at μ − ½σ², the mean at μ. With μ = 8% and σ = 20%, the median grows at 6% a year.

Part 1 · what lognormal looks like

GBM spreads outcomes wider over time and skews them upward

20,000 simulated paths, S₀ = 100, μ = 8%, σ = 20%. After a year, 90% end between $76 and $148; the median is $106.

Part 1 · estimation and limits

Maximum likelihood is a mean and a standard deviation

Under GBM daily log returns rₜ are i.i.d. normal, so the MLE is their sample moments, annualized with 252 days:

\[ \hat\sigma = \mathrm{std}(r_t)\sqrt{252},\qquad \hat\mu = 252\,\overline{r_t} + \tfrac12\hat\sigma^2 \]

The +½σ² turns the mean log return back into the drift of the SDE.

Where GBM breaks

  • Fat tails: excess kurtosis far above the normal's 0.
  • Negative skew: crashes are bigger and more frequent than a normal allows.
  • Volatility is not constant: it clusters, and it is rough (Gatheral et al., 2018).

The notebook keeps GBM, then measures the cost: Part 2 forecasts σ daily; Part 4 tests how often GBM's ranges are breached.

PART 02

Volatility forecasting

σ moves an option price more than any other input, so it is forecast fresh every day. Five models compete on the next 21 trading days.

Cells 6–7
Part 2 · what we forecast

The target: average daily variance over the option's life

\[ y_t = \frac{1}{21}\sum_{k=1}^{21} r_{t+k}^2 \]

forward_realized_variance(r, 21) = (r**2).rolling(21).mean().shift(-21). A forecast at t may use data through day t, never yₜ itself.

A better daily input: range-based realized variance

A squared return is noisy. High, low, open and close carry more information:

\[ RV_t = \ln^2\!\tfrac{O_t}{C_{t-1}} + \tfrac12\ln^2\!\tfrac{H_t}{L_t} - (2\ln2-1)\ln^2\!\tfrac{C_t}{O_t} \]

daily_variance_proxy: overnight gap + Garman-Klass. HAR uses it; the target stays close-to-close.

Part 2 · the contenders

Five forecasts of daily variance

Part 2 · the two fitted models

GARCH reverts to a long-run level; HAR blends three horizons

GARCH(1,1)

\[ h_{t+1} = \omega + \alpha r_t^2 + \beta h_t,\quad V_L=\frac{\omega}{1-\alpha-\beta} \]

MLE of −½Σ(log hₜ + r²ₜ/hₜ) under α + β < 1. The H-day average forecast has a closed form, with φ = α + β:

\[ \bar h_{t,H} = V_L + (h_{t+1}-V_L)\frac{1-\phi^H}{(1-\phi)H} \]

HAR-RV (Corsi, 2009)

The Heterogeneous Autoregressive model of Realized Volatility captures long memory with daily, weekly and monthly averages:

\[ y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_d RV_t + \beta_w \overline{RV}_{t-4:t} + \beta_m \overline{RV}_{t-21:t} + \varepsilon_t \]

HAR is the standard strong baseline; rough-volatility models can beat it.

Part 2 · honest evaluation

Refit every January on data known by then, score with QLIKE

  • GARCH: fit on all returns before the refit date; filter forward with fixed parameters.
  • HAR: a training row t counts only if its target window t+1…t+21 ended before the refit date: train = slice(0, start - horizon).
  • Design note: the GARCH recursion runs as one scipy.signal.lfilter call, not a Python loop, which matters across hundreds of likelihood evaluations.
\[ \mathrm{QLIKE}=\frac1n\sum_t\Big(\frac{y_t}{f_t}-\ln\frac{y_t}{f_t}-1\Big) \]

0 for a perfect forecast. It penalizes under-prediction more, the right asymmetry for an options seller, and is robust to proxy noise (Patton, 2011).

forecast_metrics also reports RMSE, MAE and bias in vol points, and Mincer-Zarnowitz R².

Part 2 · the cells

Cells 6–7: forecasts, accuracy and the variance risk premium

  • Cell 6 builds the RV proxy and target, runs all five forecasters into var_forecasts, keeps out-of-sample dates, and draws the metrics table sorted by QLIKE.
  • Cell 7 converts variance to annualized vol, √(f × 252), plots HAR, GARCH and VIX against realized forward vol, and measures the variance risk premium: implied minus later realized vol.

What to expect. HAR and GARCH beat historical vol on QLIKE. VIX tracks realized vol closely but sits above it on most days. That bias is the insurance premium option buyers pay, and the economic fact the agent is up against: buying at implied vol usually overpays.

PART 03

Black-Scholes, Greeks and Monte Carlo

A fair price is the expected payoff under the risk-neutral measure, discounted. Black-Scholes computes it in closed form; Monte Carlo by simulation.

Cells 8–9
Part 3 · the formula

For pricing, the real drift μ is replaced by r

\[ V_0 = e^{-rT}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\text{payoff}(S_T)],\qquad dS_t=(r-q)S_t\,dt+\sigma S_t\,dW_t^{\mathbb{Q}} \]
\[ C = Se^{-qT}N(d_1) - Ke^{-rT}N(d_2),\qquad P = Ke^{-rT}N(-d_2) - Se^{-qT}N(-d_1) \]
\[ d_1=\frac{\ln(S/K)+(r-q+\tfrac12\sigma^2)T}{\sigma\sqrt T},\qquad d_2=d_1-\sigma\sqrt T \]

N(d₂) is the risk-neutral probability the call ends in the money. Put-call parity, C − P = Se−qT − Ke−rT, holds exactly in bs_price. μ returns only in Part 5.

Part 3 · the Greeks

Delta is about ±0.5 at the money, near 0 or 1 a few dollars away

\[ \Delta_C=e^{-qT}N(d_1) \]
\[ \Gamma=\frac{e^{-qT}\varphi(d_1)}{S\sigma\sqrt T} \]
\[ \mathcal V=Se^{-qT}\varphi(d_1)\sqrt T \]

Units as a trader reads them: vega per vol point (÷100), theta per calendar day (÷365), rho per 1% (÷100). Call and put deltas differ by exactly 1; the slope at the strike is gamma.

Part 3 · Monte Carlo

Antithetic variates narrow the error band at every path count

\[ \hat V=e^{-rT}\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \text{payoff}\big(S_0e^{(r-q-\frac12\sigma^2)T+\sigma\sqrt TZ_i}\big) \]

Exact simulation in log space; error falls as 1/√n. Antithetic pairs reuse Z as −Z. The standard error must use the pair averages, or it is understated.

About 27% narrower at every n, worth roughly 1.9× as many plain paths. drift=μ switches to a real-world value (Part 5).

PART 04

Forecasting with GBM

To forecast rather than price, GBM needs the real-world drift. Then the honest question: how often does reality land inside GBM's ranges?

Cells 10–12
Part 4 · drift and calibration

A shrunk momentum drift, and a z-score test of the ranges

A drift you can defend

\[ \mu_t = s\big(252\,\bar r_{t-251:t}+\tfrac12\sigma_{f,t}^2\big)+(1-s)\,r_{f,t},\quad s=0.5 \]

The trailing term is time-series momentum (Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen). s = 0 gives a no-view forecast; s = 1 trusts momentum fully.

Calibration

\[ z_t=\frac{\ln(S_{t+21}/S_t)-(\mu_t-\tfrac12\sigma_t^2)\tau}{\sigma_t\sqrt\tau},\quad \tau=\tfrac{21}{252} \]
  • 50% inside |z| < 0.674, 90% inside |z| < 1.645, 5% in each tail.
  • PIT u = N(z) should be flat at 10% per decile. U-shaped: ranges too narrow; humped: too wide.
  • Non-overlapping windows only, or counts look far too precise.

Cells 10–12: fan chart, today's view, calibration

PART 05

The GBM edge signal

Does the market charge more or less than GBM expects the option to pay? Positive edge means the option looks cheap.

Cell 13
Part 5 · the formula

Real-world expected value reuses Black-Scholes with the rate set to μ

\[ G = e^{-rT}\,\mathbb{E}^{\mu}\big[(S_T-K)^+\big] \]

Black-Scholes with rate μ equals e−μT times that expectation under drift μ. Multiply by eμT, discount at r:

\[ G = e^{(\mu-r)T}\,\mathrm{BS}(S,K,T,\ \text{rate}=\mu,\ \sigma_f),\qquad \text{edge}=\frac{G}{\mathrm{BS}(S,K,T,r,\sigma_{IV})}-1 \]

That is gbm_expected_value, checked against Monte Carlo with drift=μ.

PART 06

Reinforcement learning: from Q-learning to DQN

The agent learns a Q-function, the expected discounted future reward of each action in each state, and acts greedily on it.

func_lib.Agent
Part 6 · the decision problem

A Markov decision process with a 20-day horizon

ElementIn this notebook
State sₜmarket changes and levels + book features
Action aₜhold, buy call, buy put, sell call, sell put, close oldest
Reward Rₜ₊₁change in equity, % of starting capital
Transitionmarket moves a day; re-mark, expire, margin call
Discount γ0.95
\[ G_t = R_{t+1}+\gamma R_{t+2}+\gamma^2R_{t+3}+\dots \]
\[ Q^*(s,a)=\mathbb E\big[R_{t+1}+\gamma\max_{a'}Q^*(s_{t+1},a')\big] \]

With γ = 0.95 the effective horizon is 1/(1 − γ) = 20 days, close to the 21-day option life.

Part 6 · deep Q-networks

A network maps a state to one Q-value per action

\[ L(\theta)=\frac1B\sum_{i=1}^{B}\Big(r_i+\gamma(1-d_i)\max_{a'}Q_{\bar\theta}(s_i',a')-Q_\theta(s_i,a_i)\Big)^2 \]

Experience replay

Up to 5,000 transitions; random mini-batches of 32 break the correlation between consecutive days.

Batched targets

Two forward passes and one train_on_batch per step; only the taken action's Q moves.

Target network

Optional target_update_every=N: a lagged copy scores the target, so the net is not chasing itself.

targets = self.q_values(states)
best_next = self._q(self.target_model or self.model, next_states).max(axis=1)
targets[np.arange(batch_size), actions] = rewards + self.gamma * best_next * (1 - dones)
loss = self.model.train_on_batch(states, targets)
Part 6 · exploration

A fixed decay stops exploring early; a schedule explores through all episodes

ε-greedy: random action with probability ε, multiplied by epsilon_decay each replay step. epsilon_decay_for(N) solves decay = (εmin/ε₀)1/(0.8N). Test mode ignores ε.

PART 07

The options book environment

The environment turns a daily choice into priced trades, enforces margin and limits, and returns the reward. An agent learns whatever the environment lets it get away with.

Cells 14–15 · run_options_episode
Part 7 · actions

Six actions, each trade one contract of a 21-day ATM option

ActionCash at entryExit (close / margin call)Margin
0 hold–––

Part 7 · the four positions

Longs risk only the premium; shorts keep at most the premium

P&L at expiry per contract of an ATM 1-month option at IV 18% (call $2.24, put $1.91). A 10% drop costs the short put about $809.

Part 7 · risk controls

Naked shorts post margin and can be forced out

\[ \text{margin}=100\times\Big(V+\max(0.20\,S-\text{OTM},\ 0.10\,S)\Big) \]

Example

Sell the ATM put at the bid (IV 17%), $1.7925. Margin = 100 × (1.7925 + max(20 − 0, 10)) = $2,179.25, about 12× the premium collected.

  • Free equity = equity − margin in use. A buy needs free equity ≥ premium; a sale needs free equity ≥ its new margin.
  • Margin call: while equity < margin in use, the oldest short is bought back at the ask.
  • Panic filter: no new shorts when the 21-day return is negative and IV is above its training 80th percentile (Daniel & Moskowitz).
Part 7 · reward and state

Reward is marked equity change; the state includes the book

\[ R_{t+1}=100\times\frac{E_{t+1}-E_t}{\text{capital}},\quad E_t=\text{cash}_t+\sum \text{sign}\times V_t\times100 \]

The spread is felt the moment a trade opens, which discourages churning. By construction final equity − capital = Σ trade P&L; this held to the cent and caught a sign bug.

\[ s_t=\big[\ \mathrm{expit}(\Delta z_t),\ \mathrm{expit}(z_t),\ b_t\ \big] \]
  • z: 12 features scaled with a train-only scaler.
  • Changes show movement; levels show whether the edge is high.
  • Book b: slots per position type, time to expiry, margin utilization. Without it the policy cannot know what it holds.

Part 8 · the core loop

Trade at t, then the market moves, then the reward is measured

for t in range(n - 1):
    action = policy(t, state) if policy else agent.act(state)      # epsilon-greedy
    # open (if allowed: slots, short cap, free equity, panic filter) or close the oldest
    # move to t+1: settle expiries, re-mark the book, force margin calls
    reward = 100 * (equity[t + 1] - equity[t]) / capital
    next_state = concat(market_states[t + 1], book_features(t + 1))
    if train:
        agent.remember(state, action, reward, next_state, done)
        agent.exp_replay(batch_size)                                 # one gradient step
    state = next_state

The order inside a step matters: every action is judged only on what happened after it was taken.

q_next_online = self.q_values(next_states)                     # choose with the online net
q_next_target = self._q(self.target_model or self.model, next_states)
best = q_next_target[np.arange(batch_size), q_next_online.argmax(axis=1)]
targets[np.arange(batch_size), actions] = rewards + self.gamma * best * (1 - dones)

Action masking: store a valid-action mask with each transition, set masked Q-values to −∞ before the max, and pass the mask to Agent.act(state, valid_actions).

Part 10A · reward and replay

Price risk into the reward; replay the informative days

Differential Sharpe ratio (Moody & Saffell, 2001)

\[ D_t=\frac{B_{t-1}\Delta A_t-\tfrac12A_{t-1}\Delta B_t}{(B_{t-1}-A_{t-1}^2)^{3/2}} \]

A, B: running first and second moments, η ≈ 0.01. Alternatives: a drawdown penalty, or a CVaR / semivariance penalty that targets negative skew.

  • n-step targets: sum n real rewards before bootstrapping; n ≈ 5–10 links a trade to its outcome faster.
  • Prioritized replay (Schaul et al.): sample ∝ |δ|α, correct with weights (Np)−β, so crash days are replayed enough.
  • With Double and Dueling DQN these form the core of Rainbow (Hessel et al., 2018).

Part 10B · intermediate models

SVI smile first; local vol and jumps reuse what exists

SVI (Gatheral)

\[ w(k)=a+b\Big(\rho(k-m)+\sqrt{(k-m)^2+\sigma^2}\Big) \]

Total implied variance by log-moneyness, five parameters per expiry. The most valuable first upgrade: OTM options at realistic prices.

Dupire local volatility

\[ \sigma_{loc}^2=\frac{\partial_T C+rK\,\partial_K C}{\tfrac12K^2\,\partial_{KK}C} \]

Merton jump-diffusion

def merton_price(S, K, T, r, sigma, lam, mu_j, sig_j,
                 kind='call', n_terms=40):
    k = np.exp(mu_j + 0.5 * sig_j ** 2) - 1
    lam_p, total = lam * (1 + k), 0.0
    for n in range(n_terms):
        sigma_n = np.sqrt(sigma ** 2 + n * sig_j ** 2 / T)
        r_n = r - lam * k + n * np.log(1 + k) / T
        weight = (np.exp(-lam_p * T) * (lam_p * T) ** n
                  / math.factorial(n))
        total += weight * bs_price(S, K, T, r_n, sigma_n, kind=kind)
    return total
Part 10B · advanced models

Heston lets variance move; rough vol makes it move like markets do

\[ dS_t=\mu S_tdt+\sqrt{v_t}S_tdW^S_t,\quad dv_t=\kappa(\theta-v_t)dt+\xi\sqrt{v_t}dW^v_t,\quad d\langle W^S,W^v\rangle=\rho\,dt \]

κ mean-reversion speed, θ long-run variance, ξ vol of vol, ρ < 0 the leverage effect behind equity skew. Feller: 2κθ > ξ².

for _ in range(n_steps):                     # full truncation
    z1 = rng.standard_normal(n)
    z2 = rho * z1 + np.sqrt(1 - rho**2) * rng.standard_normal(n)
    vp = np.maximum(v, 0)
    S *= np.exp((r - 0.5 * vp) * dt + np.sqrt(vp * dt) * z1)
    v += kappa * (theta - vp) * dt + xi * np.sqrt(vp * dt) * z2

Rough volatility

Log-variance driven by a Volterra process with H ≈ 0.1. Reproduces ATM skew growing like TH−1/2 as expiry shortens. Monte Carlo only (hybrid scheme); calibrated with a neural surrogate (Horvath, Muguruza & Tomas, 2021).

Part 11 · next steps

A seven-step roadmap, ordered by effort

  1. Quick wins (a day): target network + Double DQN, masked targets, differential-Sharpe reward, seeds with a validation split.
  2. Data realism (a week): real chain data with skew (ORATS, OptionMetrics, CBOE DataShop), SVI per expiry, OTM strikes and two expiries, commissions.
  3. Risk and sizing (a week): defined-risk spreads, and vega- or margin-based sizing.
  4. Better forecasts (a week): log-HAR, HARQ, HAR-IV, RFSV, and a combination, with DM and MCS tests.
  5. Stochastic vol and jumps (2–3 weeks): Heston and Bates calibration; a Bates-based edge.
  6. Advanced learning (ongoing): Rainbow, then QR-DQN or PPO with vega sizing, pre-trained on simulated Heston paths, several underlyings.
  7. Research frontier: rough Bergomi with neural calibration; implied-vol surface forecasts as state.
